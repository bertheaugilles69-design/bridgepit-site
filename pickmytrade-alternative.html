A PickMyTrade alternative that isn't another cloud.

PickMyTrade forwards your TradingView alerts to your broker through its servers. BridgePit does the same job with no servers in the middle: a desktop app on your own machine executes the alert, the order leaves from your own IP, and your broker login never leaves your computer. Same alerts - different architecture.

The one difference that decides it.

Cloud relay - PickMyTrade

TradingView fires your alert
…to their cloud servers, which hold your broker access
…which place the order from a datacenter IP

Local execution - BridgePit

TradingView fires your alert
…to your own computer, credentials encrypted on it
…which places the order from your own IP

Why funded traders care: firm rules on automation and third-party tools differ and change - some firms prohibit automation entirely. Always check your firm's current policy yourself. We never claim a firm has approved BridgePit.

When PickMyTrade is the better pick.

Honest answer, because it's also our sales filter: if you run many accounts on their flat $50, need a broker we don't support yet, or want nothing running at home - machine off, cloud does everything - PickMyTrade fits you better today. BridgePit exists for one trader specifically: the one whose income depends on a funded account and who doesn't want their execution living on someone else's IP.

The URL can stay. The message cannot.

  1. Install BridgePit and follow the 20-minute setup - it ends with a paper fill on your screen.
  2. Point each TradingView alert at your Mac. The webhook URL can end in /webhook or /v2/add-trade-data - both answer. That is the only part that is a URL edit.
  3. Replace the message. Do not keep the PickMyTrade body. In the app, Settings → Alert setup → Copy block. That writes the strategy name, your token, and the two clocks. Paste that into TradingView. Keep the old body and the alert is refused.
  4. Watch a day of paper fills, then cancel the old subscription when you trust what you see.

Questions traders ask.

Can I keep my existing alert format?

The URL, yes - /v2/add-trade-data still answers. The message, no. BridgePit routes by the strategy name you typed in the app, not by symbol, and it needs your BridgePit token. Copy block writes both. Keep the old body and the alert is refused.

Is BridgePit fully automated?

Semi-automated by design: it executes your TradingView alerts and you supervise. The kill-switch, per-strategy contract caps, duplicate rejection and the end-of-day flatten are on from the start. The daily loss stop is the one number you enter yourself.

Is local execution allowed at my prop firm?

BridgePit runs on your own computer and sends orders from your own IP - no cloud server between your signals and your broker. Rules differ by firm and change, so always check your firm's current policy.

What if my internet drops mid-position?

No new orders leave this Mac while it is down: TradingView cannot reach it, and it cannot reach the broker. A stop you set is already resting at the broker and still protects the open position. When the connection returns, BridgePit reconnects and the end-of-day flatten keeps asking the broker until it confirms the account is flat - and if it cannot by the cutoff, it raises the alarm rather than pretending. It is software on your machine: it runs while the Mac is awake, online and the app is open.
