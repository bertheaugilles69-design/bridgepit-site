Why it has to be local

You spent months earning that account.

A relay in the middle is three separate risks. All three land on your account, not on theirs.

Your login sits on their server

You can't see where it is stored, who can read it, or what happens to it if that company is sold. On BridgePit it never leaves your Mac.

Their downtime is your open position

If the middle is down, queued or rate-limited, the exit you sent never reaches the broker. You find out from your account, afterwards.

Orders leave from a shared address

Every customer of a cloud tool sends orders from the same handful of machines. Yours leave from your own connection, like every order you've ever placed by hand.

The rails

Every rail is here because a real day goes wrong.

None of them are settings you have to discover. They are on, and you can change or switch off any of them.

01

Duplicate guard

TradingView re-sends the same alert. BridgePit sends one order and writes down that it blocked the rest.

02

Stop resting at the broker

Your emergency stop sits with the broker, not in the app — so it still works if your Mac closes.

03

Stop for today

Lose your chosen amount and new entries stop. It resets the next morning.

04

Stop for good

Fall a set distance below your best balance and it stops for good, not just for the day.

05

End of day flatten

At the time you set, anything still open is closed and confirmed by the broker.

06

Halt and flatten

One button blocks new entries while exits still work. The next one closes everything, now.

07

Late alert gate

An alert that arrives late, after a reconnect, does not open a position at yesterday's price.

08

Awake and watched

Your Mac is kept awake while armed, and the app tells you the moment you are on battery.

Setting it up

Three steps, and a test fill before any money moves.

No config file, no terminal, nothing to edit by hand. Everything is a screen in the app.

1

Install and set a password

Open the app, choose a password for the dashboard. There is no account to create and nothing to sign into — it is running on your machine.

2

Add your broker and your strategies

Connect the broker once. Then name each strategy, pick the instrument, the number of contracts and the emergency stop distance.

3

Paste the alert block, send a test

The app writes the TradingView block for you, with your token already in it. Press Send a test signal and watch the fill land in the app.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Can you see my broker login?

No. It is encrypted on your machine and never sent anywhere. We run no server in the path between you and your broker, so there is nothing on our side to leak.

Is this allowed where I trade?

Rules differ by firm and they change. BridgePit places orders from your own machine and your own connection, and you stay at the controls. Read your firm's rules — we never claim approval on your behalf.

What if my Mac closes mid-trade?

Your emergency stop is resting at the broker, not inside the app, so it still protects the position. The app also keeps the Mac awake while it is armed and warns you on battery.

Which brokers work today?

Rithmic. More are added only once each one is proven end to end with real orders, not before.

Does it trade on its own?

It acts on the alerts you set up, on the instruments and sizes you set, and it stops the moment you press halt. It is your strategy and your session — the app is the cockpit, not the pilot.

Windows?

macOS today, on Apple Silicon. The Windows build exists and is not released yet.
