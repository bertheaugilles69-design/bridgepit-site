Daily stop
Stop for today after losing $
Today only, and it resets every morning. When today's loss reaches this, BridgePit closes that account and takes no new trades on it until tomorrow. If your firm has a daily limit, set yours a little lower so you stop before they do.
Drawdown stop
Stop for good with this much left above the floor $ below its best
Measured from the balance your firm closes the account at, which you fill in on the account itself. Get this close to it and BridgePit closes everything and stays stopped until you switch it back on. At that point you are close to losing the account, and that should be your decision, not a program's. Without that firm balance filled in, this stop cannot run and BridgePit will tell you so. This one never resets.
End of day
Close these accounts at New York time
A failsafe that runs even if your strategy's own exit alert never arrives. On US half-days BridgePit moves this earlier by itself, to 15 minutes before the early close, and warns you the day before. Prop-firm flat-by-close rules apply on half-days too. The half-day list is kept by us and can change, so check your firm's calendar too. The close is retried until your broker confirms you are flat, but if your broker is unreachable through the close, a position can stay open. Your brokerage account is not touched by this.
When one of them hits its limit
That account only: it is closed and halted, your other funded accounts keep trading. Every funded account: one limit closes and halts all of them, right if they are meant to move together. Either way this stops at the edge of this page: your brokerage account is never halted by a funded limit.
Stop at a balance, and your firm's figures
A profit target and a firm's floor, payout threshold and day count are a single account's numbers, because a 50K and a 150K do not share them. They stay on the account: open Limits & firm figures on its dashboard card.
BridgePit does not know any firm's rules and never fills one in for you. We only do the arithmetic.

Alerts cannot reach this computer yet
TradingView calls you over the public internet, and this computer has no public address yet. These four steps give it one - free, about ten minutes, once - and the last step proves the whole road with a real test alert.
  1. 1
    Install Tailscale and sign in
    A free personal account is enough - Google or Apple login works. Install it, sign in, leave it running in the background.
  2. 2
    Switch your public address on
    Copy this into Terminal (press ⌘ space, type Terminal, hit return). It answers with your public address - the line ending in .ts.net.
  3. 3
    Paste the address into BridgePit
    BridgePit adds /webhook itself, and from then on it is the address Alert setup hands you.
  4. 4
    Prove the roadpathtest alert
    First the path: can the internet reach BridgePit here? Then the real thing - a test alert leaves this machine, travels your public address, comes back in and fills on paper. The exact road every TradingView alert takes.

Replace this account

Funded accounts die. This retires the dead one and brings its replacement in without rebuilding anything.
What happens
Carried over
Every strategy that ran on it - same names, sizes and stops - its label, your firm figures, its own loss limits, its keep-alive entry.
Reset
Its peak - the new account inherits no peak; an unearned peak would poison the trailing floor. Any halt. Anything still on the dead account's book.
The replacement
If the new account came with a new login, also update the broker connection's credentials afterwards.
