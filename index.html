The rails

A day only has to go wrong once.

Here is an ordinary session, and the rail that catches each thing that goes wrong in it. You set the numbers - the app enforces them on the same machine that places the orders.

09:47
TradingView fires the same alert three times in a row.
Duplicate guardThe same signal repeated within ten seconds sends one order. The rest are refused and written into the log, so you see it happened instead of finding a doubled position.
09:47
The entry fills.
Stop at the brokerIf you set one, it is placed with the broker - not held only in the app.
11:26
Your Mac tries to go to sleep.
Awake while armedIt's held awake for as long as you're armed, and the moment you're running on battery the app says so on the dashboard.
13:58
The day turns against you.
Stop for todayAt the number you set, everything open is closed.
14:20
You want out. Now.
Halt · Close allOne button blocks new entries while your exits still work. The one beside it closes everything and waits for the broker to confirm it.
15:55
The session ends and something is still open.
End of day flattenAt the time you chose, anything still open is closed, and the app keeps asking the broker until it confirms you are flat. If it still cannot by the cutoff, it raises the alarm rather than pretending.

And across days: fall a set distance below your best end-of-day balance and BridgePit stops for good, not just for the day - the one rail that never resets by itself.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Can you see my broker login?

No. It is encrypted on your machine and never sent anywhere. We run no server in the path between you and your broker, so there is nothing on our side to leak.

Is this allowed where I trade?

Rules differ by firm and they change. BridgePit places orders from your own machine and your own connection, and you stay at the controls. Read your firm's rules - we never claim approval on your behalf.

What if I close the lid?

The Mac sleeps. BridgePit stops placing orders. That is how local works - not a cloud tool that keeps going without you. If you set a stop on that strategy, it is already at the broker and still protects the open position. Keep-awake only stops idle sleep while the lid is open.

What if the internet drops?

Nothing trades. TradingView cannot reach the Mac, and the Mac cannot reach the broker. New alerts do not arrive, new orders do not leave, and flatten cannot fire until the connection is back. If you set a stop on that strategy, it is already at the broker and still protects the open position. The Mac has to stay on the internet the whole session - same rule as the lid.

Which brokers work today?

Rithmic. BridgePit passed Rithmic's vendor conformance in July 2026. More brokers are added only once each one is proven end to end with real orders, not before.

Does it trade on its own?

It acts on the alerts you set up, on the instruments and sizes you set, and it stops the moment you press halt. It is your strategy and your session - the app is the cockpit, not the pilot.

Can I try it without handing over my broker login?

Yes, and you should. Paper mode is free and needs no card and no broker connection. Alerts arrive, orders fill on paper, and you watch a whole session before deciding anything.

How do I cancel?

Send one email and it is cancelled. There is no retention call and nothing to argue with. Your app keeps working on paper afterwards.

Windows?

macOS today, on Apple Silicon. A Windows version is planned, and it is not released yet.

Run one session on paper, free.

No BridgePit account and no card. It is a Mac app, and it stops when you close it.
