MyFundedFutures and automated trading: what their rules actually say.

Not what a forum says. Below is each relevant rule in MyFundedFutures' own words, linked to their help centre, followed by what it means if you execute your own TradingView alerts from your own machine. Their rules change — check the source links before you act on any of it.

First, the thing every prop firm asks about.

The objection that kills most execution tools at a prop firm is copy trading, and firms are right to be strict about it. So, plainly:

What BridgePit is not

Not a copier. Not signal replication. Not a managed service. No trade is ever copied from one person onto another person's account, and your strategy is never transmitted to anyone — not to other traders, not to us.

What it is

An execution bridge. Your own chart strategy fires an alert; software on your computer places that order on your account with your credentials, from your own IP address. You watch it, and you can stop it instantly.

Rule one

Traders may not copy each other.

"Traders are not permitted to copy trade one another by entering, exiting or cancelling trade positions." MyFundedFutures — Fair Play and Prohibited Trading Practices
"Collaborating with others to execute identical or opposite strategies across unconnected accounts is prohibited." Same article

Both rules describe trades moving between different people. That is the category an execution bridge sits outside of: one trader, one strategy, one account, no replication to anyone else. Note MyFundedFutures separately publishes a copy-trading policy of its own, naming a preferred copier partner and stating that "external copy trading solutions can also be used" — so third-party execution software is not foreign to them. Their copy-trading article →

Rule two

Automated strategies are permitted, with two conditions.

Their fair-play article permits traders to use automated strategies configured to their own settings, provided the tool does not aim to exploit the favourable fills of the simulated environment, and states that live automated trading must follow CME guidelines. Read the current wording yourself — this is the clause most likely to be updated. Source →

What that asks of a tool: it should place the orders your strategy asks for, at the pace you actually trade, with nothing engineered around simulated-fill behaviour.

Rule three

High-frequency trading is out.

"High-frequency Trading is not allowed on our plans." MyFundedFutures — Fair Play and Prohibited Trading Practices

Alert-driven execution is the opposite end of that spectrum. Your chart fires a signal; one order goes in. That is typically a handful of trades a day — the same rhythm you already trade by hand, minus the typing.

Rule four

One trader, one device.

"Each individual trader may not use the same device (tablet, phone or computer) as used by another trader." MyFundedFutures — Fair Play and Prohibited Trading Practices

Worth reading next to how cloud execution works. A hosted service runs your orders from its infrastructure alongside everyone else's. Local execution runs on the machine you already trade from, under your own account, from your own IP — which is the arrangement this rule is written around.
